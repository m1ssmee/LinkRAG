T1: Intra-Video Temporal-Aware RAG on LectQA-Vid

Their claimWhat we foundVerdict
Open-ended answers reach F1 23.52 and similarity 74.42 (Table 4). Their answering model is unnamed, so the number cannot be re-run. Repeating the question as the answer scores 22.87 F1 on our 300-question subset, and 23.52 sits at that floor. With one named answerer, our rebuild of their pipeline scores 31.23. Not reproducible; at the floor
Multiple-choice accuracy 53.43 (Table 5). The correct option is A in 99.7 % of the published MCQs and the unique longest option in 94.6 %. With options shuffled, our answerer scores 95.33; picking the longest option without the video scores 94.4. MCQ half measures neither the video nor retrieval
Timestamp-aware retrieval beats multimodal RAG without timestamps, F1 23.52 against 14.80 (Table 6). In our rebuild, switching the temporal filter off raises F1 from 31.23 to 34.69. Where the filter keeps some evidence it makes no measurable difference (−0.53 [−1.30, +0.20]). Not reproduced
Removing the temporal filter drops F1 to 11.40, the largest effect in their ablation (Table 7). The filter left 34 of 160 questions with no context, which accounts for 2.93 of the 3.46-point loss with it on. In all 34 a matching chunk existed and was cut before the filter ran. The paper does not say where the filter's interval comes from at question time, or what happens when nothing survives; our rebuild uses the annotated interval. Opposite direction in our rebuild
Retrieval with temporal constraints is the way to answer questions about a lecture video. The whole transcript beats every retrieval mode: +2.04 F1 [+1.08, +3.06] over our best. Every transcript is under about 1,550 tokens, and all 95 videos together are 72,926, so retrieval can only lose information here. The benchmark cannot show retrieval's value
LectQA-Vid: 100 videos, 3,000 time-stamped QA pairs, an 80/10/10 split and a 1,000-pair evaluation subset. 95 videos are still available. The intervals of 1,598 of the 2,832 questions on them pass format and range checks. The split and the evaluation subset are not published. Partly usable

Our claim on T1. With the same answerer for every method, our retrieval with one re-query scores +2.00 token-F1 [+0.96, +3.11] above a controlled re-implementation of T1's pipeline with its temporal filter off. The re-query, a technique from MI-RAG, carries +1.74 of it; without the re-query the two retrieval pipelines are level (+0.27 [−0.90, +1.48]). By difficulty: simple +2.91, hard +1.05 (interval contains 0), very hard +2.06.

T2: MaViLS

Their claimWhat we foundVerdict
The audio transcript alone aligns at F1 0.53 (Table 1). Their similarity with their decoder, re-run by us: 0.513 over all 20 lectures. Our decoder on the same matrix: 0.520. Reproduced within 0.02
Combining OCR, image and transcript features with a jump-penalised DP reaches 0.82 (Table 2). On the same 9 test lectures (0.80 there), our image, frame-OCR and speech-text fusion reaches 0.853: +0.055 [−0.050, +0.161], 5 wins, 1 tie, 3 losses. Holding out each of 19 lectures in turn: +0.050 [+0.002, +0.103], 9 wins, 2 ties, 8 losses, with three lectures carrying the difference. Parity
The dynamic program with a slide-jump penalty is the core of the method. On our features, their DP and ours are level (−0.004 [−0.015, +0.007] over 19 lectures). Either adds about 0.06 over taking each sentence's best slide. Matters, but no decoder wins
The features complement each other: OCR alone 0.76, image alone 0.64 (Table 1). Frame OCR alone 0.818, image alone 0.730, speech text alone 0.483 on test. Dropping the speech text costs 0.011, which is not measurable. Confirmed: the frames carry it
Hard lectures: Solar resource 0.53, Sensory systems 0.53, Phonetics 0.65. We score 0.27 to 0.34 higher on these three. We lose on camera-heavy lectures: Short range −0.22, Reinforcement −0.08. Different strengths

Our claim on T2. Combining speech-to-slide text, frame OCR and image features, taken from the frame at each sentence's timestamp, our alignment reaches a mean F1 of 0.853 on the 9 held-out lectures with video, against 0.80 for MaViLS's published all-features result on the same lectures. The paired difference is within lecture-to-lecture variation, so we report parity, not an improvement.

What we do not claim

  • No win over T1's published numbers: our answerer differs from theirs, and their evaluation subset is unpublished.
  • No win over MaViLS: the interval contains 0 on the registered split.
  • No benefit from LinkRAG's evidence links or complementarity reranker: neither improved retrieval on either benchmark, and cross-file links cannot be tested on them.
  • No MCQ accuracy on LectQA-Vid, for the reasons above.

LinkRAG: results on the two target benchmarks

Detailed report · 12 November 2026

Every number below is read from a stored result file in the repository (commit 29db7a9); the file is named next to each table. The re-checks of 12 November used stored answers only and cost $0.

In one paragraph. LinkRAG was measured against two published systems on their own benchmarks. On LectQA-Vid (target T1), a controlled re-implementation of T1's pipeline is beaten by our retrieval with one re-query by +2.00 token-F1 [+0.96, +3.11]; most of that (+1.74) is the re-query, a technique taken from MI-RAG. The benchmark itself cannot measure retrieval: the whole transcript beats every retrieval mode, repeating the question as the answer already scores 22.87 F1 against T1's published 23.52, and the multiple-choice half is answerable from option length alone. On MaViLS (target T2), our slide alignment reaches 0.853 against their 0.80 on the same 9 test lectures, a paired difference of +0.055 [−0.050, +0.161]: parity, carried by the video frames. LinkRAG's own components, the evidence links and the complementarity reranker, show no measured benefit on either benchmark.

1. Caveats

  • T1 is a re-implementation, not their code. T1's code, its 80/10/10 split and its 1,000-pair evaluation subset are unpublished. Where the paper is silent (the re-rank pool, context size, merge gap, cross-encoder, OCR in place of their frame captions) the choices are ours. T1's answering model is unnamed, so our absolute numbers are not comparable with their Table 4; only differences between our own rows are.
  • T1 coverage. 95 of 100 videos could still be downloaded. Open-ended answers run on a stratified subset of 300 questions (100 per difficulty, recorded seed) from 93 videos, 3 repeats each. The 1,414 multiple-choice questions ran once.
  • T1 gold timestamps. The intervals of 1,598 of 2,832 questions pass our format and range checks. None has been checked by hand; a 75-row sheet for a second person is prepared.
  • T2 coverage and exposure. 9 test lectures: the dataset release has no video for a tenth (Climate policies). Every weight and threshold was set on the 10-lecture tune half, but the test half has now been looked at four times. Their numbers are their published per-lecture figures, not re-run.
  • Statistics. All intervals are 95 % bootstrap intervals, 10,000 resamples, recorded seeds. Paired intervals resample the unit that was sampled: whole videos on LectQA-Vid, whole lectures on MaViLS. An interval that contains 0 is reported as no measurable difference.
  • Faithfulness used one judge run per claim, so it is direction only.
  • The pooled-corpus run (§3.8) is exploratory. It is outside the agreed plan and has not been agreed with the mentor.

3.5 T1's temporal filter (eq. 22)

T1 applies eq. 22 to its top-K candidates and keeps only those inside the question's time interval ± 3 s. The paper does not say where that interval comes from at question time, nor what happens when nothing survives. Our replica gives the filter the annotated interval and answers an empty result with a fixed "no evidence" reply; both are our choices. The filter applied to 160 of the 300 questions and left 34 of them with no context.

3.6 Faithfulness

Every claim in an answer was checked against the text of the units it cites, with a quotable span required (lectqa_faithfulness.md, 647–796 claims per mode). Unsupported claims: T1 replica 3.7 %, ours with re-query 3.1 %, full transcript 4.3 %. The largest gap is inside the standard error of a difference, so no mode is distinguishable. The full transcript's F1 gain is not paid for in unsupported claims.

The interval metric cannot reward evidence from another modality, and 2,102 of the 2,832 questions refer to "the video" or "the speaker", so the setting is a stress test rather than a benchmark.

4. T2: MaViLS

4.1 Protocol

Their scorer, verbatim from their repository: one prediction per transcript sentence, scikit-learn micro F1 over sentences whose label is not −1, with −1 kept in the label set; the paper's averages are unweighted means over lectures. The 20 lectures were split once (seed 20260922) into a 10-lecture tune half, the only place any parameter was set, and a 10-lecture test half. One test lecture has no video in their release, so every test mean is over 9 lectures and paired with their published figure for the same lecture.

5. LinkRAG's own components

  • Evidence links. No link variant measurably improved localisation on LectQA-Vid: temporal links, links over frame-derived slides, or links with the reranker. Cross-file links, the case the layer was designed for, cannot be tested on either benchmark, because neither has evidence that spans files.
  • Complementarity reranker. On normalised scores it lowers hit@3 by about 5 points on LectQA-Vid. A defect was found on 12 November: in baseline and iterative mode it received raw fusion scores of about 0.03 next to bonus and penalty weights of 0.3 and 0.4, so relevance barely counted. The rows measured that way, including an earlier "−13 points of hit@3", are retired as not valid estimates. Fixing the scale is a design change that has not been made.
  • Slide alignment, the one linking component with a reported number, is the T2 result above.

6. Contributions as they stand

  1. Controlled re-implementation of T1 and a corrected comparison. One answerer for every mode; ours with one re-query +2.00 F1 [+0.96, +3.11] over T1's pipeline with its filter off, +1.74 of it from the re-query; the full transcript beats every retrieval mode.
  2. Benchmark audit of LectQA-Vid. MCQs answerable from option position and length; a question-echo floor of 22.87 F1 against T1's 23.52; 1,598 of 2,832 intervals pass format and range checks; every transcript fits in one context.
  3. Multi-feature slide alignment at parity with MaViLS: 0.853 against 0.80 on the same 9 test lectures, +0.055 [−0.050, +0.161]; the frames carry it.
  4. Claim-level verification: every claim checked against its cited evidence; unsupported-claim rates of 3.1–4.3 %, direction only.

7. Open items

  • Hand-check of the gold timestamps by a second person, using the prepared 75-row sheet.
  • Closed-book control (proposed, about $1.20, not approved): the same 300 questions answered with no context and with the full transcript under one prompt that allows the model's own knowledge, to measure how much LectQA-Vid can be answered without the video.
  • Reranker score scale: a config-switched fix, then one real run, if the component is to be reported again.

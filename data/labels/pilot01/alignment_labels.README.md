# pilot01 alignment labels — provenance

## Current: ear-labelled (authoritative)

- `pilot01_slide_timeline.csv` — **hand-made by the project owner while listening to
  the recording**: start/end per slide in mm:ss, with a notes column flagging inferred
  ends, zero-length slides and the Q&A outro.
- `pilot01_alignment_labels.csv` — derived per-segment truth for all 51 frozen audio
  segments, generated by `scripts/make_alignment_labels.py`. Regenerate after any
  re-segmentation; do not hand-edit.

Rules applied when converting the timeline to per-segment labels:

- A segment's true slide is the one covering the **majority of its duration**.
- Slides **9, 11, 18** are zero-length (skipped or flashed) and can never be the
  majority slide; a segment straddling one takes the neighbour covering most of it and
  is marked `ambiguous`.
- Segments with >50% of their duration in the outro (19:05–22:51) get
  `true_slide=none`, `section=qa`.
- Segments starting or ending within **±3 s of any slide boundary** are marked
  `ambiguous`, because the timeline was read by ear.

**Accuracy: approximate, ±3 s.** 51 segments → 42 talk (15 ambiguous), 9 Q&A.
23 of 27 slides are showable (slide 27 never shown; 9/11/18 zero-length). The labelled
sequence is monotonic non-decreasing, which independently confirms the assumption the
alignment DP is built on.

## Superseded: text-anchored labels (removed)

An earlier `alignment_labels.csv` held 11 rows derived by matching speech against
content unique to one slide. It was a stopgap because nobody had listened to the
recording; it was biased toward the results section, where the speaker reads numbers
aloud, and it derived from text overlap — one of the three signals the aligner itself
scores on. The ear labels supersede it entirely.

An attempt to generate labels fully automatically was tried and discarded: it mapped
the opening title segment to slide 6 on the strength of the words *goal* and *over*.
